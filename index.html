Why Forge? — Maybe the 750-Meter Walk Was Worth It

I had heard about Forge long before I actually became a part of it.

I knew about the internships. I had heard about students getting opportunities there. I knew Forge had a certain brand value and that being associated with it could add some weight to your resume. But honestly, I had no idea how I could actually get into Forge.

And by the time I started looking seriously, I thought I had already missed my chance.

One of my friends had already been selected into Forge as part of the Asari cohort, and I remember thinking, “Okay, I guess that opportunity is gone.” I wasn't even aware that the selections for the next programme were happening.

Then, one afternoon, probably around lunch, I was casually scrolling through Teams when I came across a message from Sivakumar Sir saying that the programme was open to participants across KCT and K-Class.

And that one line — “K-Class” — caught my attention.

I come from a technical background, and the thought of being around people from completely different disciplines immediately interested me. I thought, Why not? Maybe this could be an opportunity to step outside the usual engineering classroom and actually learn from people who think differently from me.

So I did the most straightforward thing possible.

I asked my friend about the programme.

She told me, “Go meet Ram Sir directly.”

And that's how my Forge journey technically began.

The funny part?

The entire journey from my classroom to Forge was probably just 500–750 metres.

That's it.

A short walk.

But sometimes, a small decision and a short walk can take you somewhere you didn't expect.

I decided to go.

I met Ram Sir, asked about the programme, and he told me to attend the interview the next day.

The next day, I attended the interview, hoping I had done well enough.

And then came the message I was waiting for:

I got selected.

And suddenly, the opportunity I thought I had missed was actually right in front of me.

What was I looking for?

When I joined Forge, I had a few expectations.

The first was interdisciplinary learning.

I didn't want to spend another experience surrounded only by people who think the way I do or come from the same technical background. I wanted to understand how people from different disciplines approach problems, how they communicate their ideas, and how they build solutions differently.

The second — and probably the most important for me — was networking.

Not networking just for the sake of collecting contacts, but meeting people, listening to their stories, understanding what they are working on, and building relationships with people who could become collaborators, mentors, friends, or even future teammates.

Then came industrial exposure.

I wanted to understand what happens beyond the classroom — how ideas are transformed into something useful, how industries look at problems, and how innovation actually works when there are real constraints, real users, and real consequences.

And finally, entrepreneurship.

This one is personal.

Somewhere five or six years down the line, I don't just want to be someone working on someone else's idea. I genuinely want to build something of my own.

I don't know exactly what that venture will be yet.

And honestly, I think that's okay.

What I do know is that I want to create something, take an idea from “What if?” to “Let's build it,” and eventually turn it into something that creates value.

For me, Forge feels like a stepping stone towards that version of myself.

Maybe I joined because I happened to see a Teams message at lunch.

Maybe I joined because my friend told me to meet Ram Sir.

Maybe I joined because Forge had a name I already knew.

Or maybe I joined because, at the right moment, I decided to take that 750-metre walk instead of scrolling past the opportunity.

Whatever the reason, I'm glad I walked.

Because sometimes, the biggest journeys don't begin with a flight ticket, a grand plan, or a perfectly designed roadmap.

Sometimes, they begin with a message on Teams...

and a decision to just go and ask.

And that's why I want to be here at Forge — to learn differently, meet differently, think differently, and hopefully, one day, build differently.
